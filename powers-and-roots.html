Exponents, Roots, and Rational Powers

In mathematics, the concept of exponents begins with a simple and intuitive definition and then gradually extends into a broader and more powerful system that includes roots, fractional powers, and negative powers. Understanding this progression is essential because exponents appear throughout algebra, calculus, the sciences, and financial mathematics. The following discussion develops these ideas carefully, beginning with positive integer exponents and concluding with the rules for zero and negative exponents.

To move beyond integer exponents, it is necessary to consider roots. Suppose aa is a positive real number and nn is a positive integer. As part of the properties of real numbers, it is assumed that there exists a unique positive real number rr such that rn=arn=a. This number rr is called the nnth root of aa and is denoted by a1/na1/n. The nnth root generalizes the existence and uniqueness of the square root, which is the familiar case where n=2n=2. For instance, the square root of 99 is 33 because 32=932=9, and similarly, the cube root of 88 is 22 because 23=823=8. This principle ensures that every positive real number has exactly one positive nnth root for each positive integer nn.

The concept of the nnth root can be further generalized to fractional powers.
